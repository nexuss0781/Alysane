## Conclusions

1. **Internal observables can support self-knowledge geometry, but not general competence identification.**
   Hidden states, logits, predictive distributions, entropy, answers, and self-consistency are internally observable. They do not determine external success when the evaluator or environment contains hidden state.

2. **Actual competence is not identifiable from internal signals alone in general.**
   A deterministic model can produce the same answer, trace, and entropy in two worlds, while one evaluator accepts the answer and another rejects it. Competence is `1` in one world and `0` in the other. Any internal-only estimator has worst-case absolute error at least `0.5` and squared error at least `0.25` on this two-world problem.

3. **Internal-only competence is possible under a sound internal verifier.**
   If success is a known measurable function of internal observables, `Y = g(O)`, competence is internally computable. This identifies verifier-success; actual task-success additionally requires verifier soundness.

4. **Minimum outcome evidence is task/answer/evaluator success, not domain labels.**
   Deterministic pointwise competence requires one verified success bit for the exact task, answer, and evaluator. Stochastic or population competence requires repeated verified outcomes and a confidence bound. For binary population estimation with `ε = δ = 0.05`, Hoeffding gives `n = 738` sufficient samples.

5. **Uncertainty is not correctness or competence.**
   A model can be certain and wrong, or uncertain and correct. The same predictive entropy can correspond to different competence. The same predictive competence can correspond to different epistemic/aleatoric decompositions.

6. **No finite evidence identifies competence on uncovered tasks without assumptions.**
   Two worlds can agree on all observed outcomes and internal observations but differ on an unobserved task. Transfer or generalization claims must be labeled as extrapolations unless supported by coverage or explicit assumptions.

## Assumptions

- Success is binary unless explicitly generalized to loss.
- Internal observables are measurable functions of the model computation.
- External evaluator/world state may contain information not present in internal observables.
- Outcome evidence is trusted or generated by a sound executable verifier.
- Hoeffding bounds assume i.i.d. bounded binary outcomes, or an explicitly stated sampling/reweighting protocol.
